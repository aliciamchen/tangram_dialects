# Figures

The article's figures are drawn by the modules in `report/figures/`, one module per figure, each named for what it shows (`group_specificity.py`, never a figure number, because the numbers change while the article is written and the manuscript's `\includegraphics` maps the number to the name). `report/plot_style.py` is the one description of how the figures look (palette, labels, type sizes, mark styles, figure and panel sizes, the broken block axis) and `report/figures/_common.py` holds the reading, the two summaries over games and the drawing idioms built on it; a module defines none of its own, so a change in either changes every figure. A figure the article no longer needs is removed with its module and its PDF; the modules are the figures the article uses, not a library of past ones.

## The contract

A module defines `data(dirs)`, the long table the figure plots, read from the processed tables, the measures, the model inputs in `data/<dataset>/model_inputs/` and the estimates in `results/estimates/`, and `plot(df)`, the figure drawn from that table alone. An aggregate a model also uses belongs in preprocessing, not here. A module whose panels the SI draws again on a subset of games gives `data()` an optional `restriction`, the name of a rerun of `stats/primary/robustness.R` as `primary/robustness.csv` names it (`RESTRICTIONS` in `_common.py`, so far `complete_games`): the table then holds the games the rerun keeps and the tests' rows are the rerun's; the runner itself always calls `data(dirs)`. The runner (`make figures`, or `uv run python -m report.figures --dataset full [name ...]`) calls both for every module and writes `results/plots/<name>.pdf` with no creation date, so an unchanged figure is byte-identical from run to run and `git diff results/plots/` is the regression check; `make sync` copies the PDFs into the manuscript's project. A figure the author assembles by hand returns a dict from `plot()`, one figure per panel keyed by what the panel shows, written as `results/plots/<name>_<key>.pdf`; a module that sets `FOLDER` writes into that subfolder instead (`confirmatory_tests/` for the figures of the preregistered tests), and `make sync` copies the subfolders too. The tests are in `report/test_figures.py`.

## The look

Uncertainty is a percentile-bootstrap 95% interval over games (`report/bootstrap.py` resamples games and pools their rows, so the point estimate is the mean over observations; the semantic-drift interval in `exploratory.tex` is one), with the games as thin lines or points behind the mean, also where a game's per-block means are noisy (the description-property trajectories, about eighteen descriptions per game and block; the convention-alignment trajectories, about fifty), whose y ranges hold every game's line. A cumulative distribution (the continuity panel) is pooled over a condition's pairs with no interval, each game's own distribution a thin line behind it. No gridlines, open black axes, one plot per figure, sentence-case labels, and no key on a panel: the author adds one legend per figure in Illustrator and resizes the panels there, so a panel names no condition, line style or category and reserves no room for one, its PDF trimmed to what it draws. The one exception among the panels is one whose lines differ by their names alone, the alignment outcomes, which names its lines with `label_lines()`; `label_conditions()`, `label_bundles()` and `text_legend()` are unused. The SI figures (`attrition_timelines.py`, `attrition_curves.py`, `convention_fates.py`, `listener_answer_rates.py`) are complete figures rather than panels, and each draws its condition names and its keys or direct labels itself; `intact_games.py`, the SI's redrawing of the main panels on the complete games, is the exception, since its panels are the main modules' own, drawn under the restriction, and follow the main panels' rules. A panel keeps its axis and tick labels, reference-line labels, and text that is data, such as the conventions written beside the example strands. The runner trims every PDF to its contents with a thin pad (`bbox_inches="tight"`, `pad_inches=0.05`), counting every axis label in full, since the tight bounding box alone would cut a label longer than its axis. Game counts are left to the caption, never written into a label or a legend; where a caption gives one it is written as "16 games", never "n = 16", which readers take as participants. Never draw a line for a quantity a condition does not have: `refer_separated` has no out-group speakers. The game-block axis is broken between the phases (`add_phase_boundary()`), each phase drawn as its own segment so that no line crosses the gap.

## Panels assembled by hand

The author assembles multi-panel figures in Illustrator from single-panel PDFs. Such panels share a plotting area through `panel_figure()`: `PANEL_AXES` for a panel of a row, `BLOCK_PANEL_AXES` for one that spans all twelve blocks, ticked with `SPARSE_BLOCK_TICKS`, `LARGE_BLOCK_PANEL_AXES`, larger and ticked at every block, for a block panel that leads its figure (the similarity trajectories of `group_specificity.py`), enlarged a little when placed so that its type reads a little larger than that of the panels beside it, and `SQUARE_PANEL_AXES` for a panel of Phase 2 outcomes, one quantity against another with a point per game or a trajectory over blocks 7 to 12 alone (`PHASE2_RANGE`, ticked with `PHASE2_TICKS`, a plain axis rather than the broken one with an empty Phase 1); the panel is small and the type is at its usual size, so that the type survives the panel being scaled down in print. A panel carries no letter, no note and no title of any kind, since the letters, notes and keys are added in Illustrator and the legend says which game and condition a panel shows; the margins hold only what the panel carries outside the axes. The panels of a row share their plotting area: the block panels are wider than tall, the Phase 2 panels square.

## Settled conventions

Applied in `convention_examples.py`, `group_specificity.py`, `block_trajectories.py`, `group_recognition.py`, `continuity.py`, `specificity_endpoints.py`, `accuracy_vs_specificity.py`, `convention_alignment.py`, `contact_network.py`, `convention_spread.py`, `convention_use.py`, `convention_maps.py`, `recognition_by_similarity.py`, `response_times.py`, `measure_agreement.py`, `permutation_null.py`, `exit_survey.py`, `listener_screens.py`, `attrition_timelines.py`, `attrition_curves.py`, `specificity_by_tangram.py`, `convention_fates.py`, `listener_answer_rates.py` and `intact_games.py`:

- Verbatim descriptions in a figure are wrapped over lines, never cut.
- Marks are filled before the manipulation and hollow after it (`PHASE_POINT`, or `ROW_POINT` on a figure with one row per game): the face the series color or white, the edge the series color.
- On the broken block axis, marks set side by side within a block are dodged with `dodge()`, by points, never by a fraction of a block, which the gap would stretch.
- A stimulus drawn in a panel (`stimulus_glyph()`) sits in headroom reserved above the data, where no mark or label can reach it.
- Where two kinds of line share a panel and the color carries the condition (within- and between-group similarity, in-group and out-group speakers), the first is solid and the second is `BETWEEN_LINESTYLE`. Where such lines carry points, the second kind's are filled triangles (`BETWEEN_MARKER`), never hollow circles, which mean Phase 2.
- The convention labels of the example strands (`convention_examples.py`), several lines each and wrapped at `LABEL_CHARS`, are each set as near its line's end as the space allows, in whichever order moves them least, since two strands often end level.
- A pair of example games is matched on the phase before the manipulation, with the rule stated in the legend, and every game of the two conditions is drawn as thin lines behind the examples' curves. The pairs are fixed in `convention_examples.py` (`EXAMPLES`) and every other example panel draws from them, so that one game is followed across figures: the contact network shows the first pair's refer_mixed game, the convention spread each pair's refer_mixed game (keyed `pair<i>_block<b>`), the convention maps both games of each pair (keyed `pair<i>_<condition>`).
- A within-group similarity mean is drawn only where at least `MIN_GAMES` games contribute (`group_specificity.py`), which omits the few block 7 pairs from games that lost a player.
- One quantity against another over games (`draw_scatter()` on a `scatter_table()`) sets a point per game behind each condition's mean, a larger point with its two intervals as a cross; where the conditions' points overlap (`specificity_endpoints.py`), `shapes=True` gives each condition its shape from `CONDITION_MARKERS` as well as its color, with the game points a little larger (`SHAPED_GAME_POINT`) so the shapes read in print, while lines never carry markers; the scatters carry no fitted lines; the Phase 2 scatter panels share the group-specificity axis `SPECIFICITY_LIMITS`, and a game appears only with both quantities.
- A per-game group-specificity endpoint comes from `specificity_endpoints()`, the sentence-embedding estimates of `stats/primary/group_specificity.R` where estimable, never recomputed.
- A measure a figure computes from the embeddings (`utterance_embeddings()`, the convention-alignment panels) is descriptive and says so in its docstring, as are the two codings of the Phase 1 conventions in `_common.py`: `phase1_conventions()`, a group's convention for a tangram as the unit mean embedding of its members' final Phase 1 descriptions (the convention-alignment panels, `recognition_by_similarity.py`), and the word coding `convention_vocabulary()` and `code_convention_use()`, a group's convention words being the content words in at least two of its members' final Phase 1 descriptions that no other group of the game has, and a description using its own group's words, both, another group's or neither (`convention_use.py`, `convention_spread.py`). A measure a model also uses belongs in preprocessing.
- A network of a game's players sets them on a circle in group order with `network_layout()`, the same layout in every panel of the same game, a node per player in its group's color (`NETWORK_NODE`, or `RINGED_NODE` where the rim carries the group and the face something else) and edges of one width in translucent ink (`NETWORK_EDGE`); what an edge counts goes in the legend, never in its width, and a node carries no label. Where the other players could not see a player's identity (Phase 2 of a mixed condition, names and avatars masked), the node is gray with a dotted ring in its group's color (`MASKED_NODE`). A sequence of mixing is drawn in the same layout, one panel per trial with each trio a triangle of edges, over consecutive trials whose trios change at every step, since about a quarter of consecutive Phase 2 trials repeat the trios of the one before, and in which no trio is three neighbors on the circle, which would read as an unmixed Phase 1 triad.
- A figure of a preregistered test (`confirmatory_h1_h2.py`, `confirmatory_h3.py`, `confirmatory_h4.py`, in `confirmatory_tests/`) shows the data the test models, one point per game in a column per condition (`columns_panel_axes()`) under the condition's mean and bootstrap interval, the planned contrast as a bracket marked by `significance_stars()` from the estimates' two-tailed p-value (*** below .001, ** below .01, * below .05, n.s.), and where the contrasts share a scale a panel of them with their model intervals (`draw_contrasts()`, odds ratios on a log axis), labelled by their predicted direction ("Separated > mixed"). H1/H2's columns are adjusted to the mean Phase 1 with the model's slope from `h1_h2_coefficients.csv`; nothing is refit. Its adjusted and before/after panels add `social_first` on the right for comparison, adjusted the same way but outside the model; the adjusted panel's bracket between `social_mixed` and `social_first` is the exploratory test of `stats/exploratory/social_phase2_specificity.R`, which fits its own Phase 1 slope. `confirmatory_summary.py` puts every test on one scale, the standardized difference between the per-game values of its two conditions (pooled within-condition SD, signed so positive is the prediction), as rows with bootstrap intervals and as columns of games measured from the comparison condition's mean; the scale is descriptive and the stars stay the tests'.
- A panel drawn again on a subset of games (`intact_games.py`, the complete-games rerun of `stats/primary/robustness.R`, written to `intact_games/`) is its source module's panel from `data(dirs, restriction=...)`: the same code on the games the rerun keeps, which `intact_games()` codes as the pipeline does (`complete_nine` of `report/descriptives.py`) and the test modules check against the rerun's game counts. Its brackets and contrasts are the rerun's, read from `primary/robustness.csv` by `contrast_estimates()`; the exploratory `social_first` bracket is its own script's rerun on the same games (`exploratory/social_phase2_specificity_robustness.csv`, named to `contrast_estimates()` as `reruns`); the H3 tests are refit on the same games by the complete-games section of `robustness.R`, so every row of the intact forest plot carries a mark; a test no script reruns carries no mark rather than the full sample's, which is why `significance_stars()` refuses a missing p-value. The adjusted H1/H2 columns use the primary model's Phase 1 slope about the kept games' mean, since the rerun writes no slope. The forest plot's axis keeps the article's range and widens only where a subset's interval runs past it, so the article's panel is unchanged.
- Stacked shares over blocks are areas in steps of the condition's color (`STACK_ALPHAS`), divided by thin white lines and spanning the blocks edge to edge, and carry no interval.
- A figure with one row per game uses the tall `ROWS_PANEL_AXES`, with its type a step larger than the other panels' (axis labels at `TITLE_SIZE`, ticks at `LABEL_SIZE`) because it is reduced further to sit beside them, orders the rows by the quantity its y label names, leaves them unlabeled and draws a permutation range as a light band (`NULL_BAND`) behind the points: in `permutation_null.py` the preregistered per-game estimates with the central 95% of the Phase 1 check's thousand permuted coefficients (`perm_lower`, `perm_upper` in `checks/phase1_group_specificity.csv`, taken from the cached permutations), never a figure-side recomputation.
- A figure with one row per target tangram (`specificity_by_tangram.py`) uses `TANGRAM_ROWS_PANEL_AXES` with its type at the usual size, labels each row with its tangram (`stimulus_row_labels()`, a gray glyph left of the axis) and orders the rows by the quantity its y label names. Within a row, its marks are set one above another a fixed step apart: the pooled Phase 1 value first, a filled point in ink, then each condition's Phase 2 value in `CONDITION_ORDER`, a hollow point (`ROW_POINT`) in the condition's color, each with its bootstrap interval as a line, and the gap between rows is wider than the step so that the tangrams read as groups. Its per-tangram specificity is descriptive, within-group minus between-group similarity of the final descriptions, since the preregistered estimate has no per-tangram value.
- A column per condition (`draw_columns()` on `COLUMNS_PANEL_AXES`) holds respondents or games: games as `GAME_POINT`, jittered across only (the word-use per-game shares), respondents as below.
- Individual respondents are jittered points lighter than game points (`RESPONDENT_POINT`) on `COLUMNS_PANEL_AXES`, the jitter seeded so the PDF reproduces, under the condition's mean as a black line across the column and its interval as the outline of a box around the line (`RESPONDENT_MEAN`, `RESPONDENT_INTERVAL`), with the condition names as the tick labels in ink; the y axis is the item's question as the survey asked it, wrapped, with the scale's ends named at its first and last ticks, copied from `ExitSurvey.jsx` and checked against it when the figure is built.
- A reference line's label sits above its right end, or its left end (`reference_line(..., side="left")`) where the data reach the right one.
- A player's time in the game (`attrition_timelines.py`, a panel per condition on `ATTRITION_PANEL_AXES`) is a bar in the original group's color (`PLAYER_BAR`) from the first block to the trial after which the server removed the player, in game-block units at trial resolution (six trials per block), or to the end of the game; a removed player's end carries the reason as a mark in ink (`REMOVAL_MARKERS`, `REMOVAL_MARK`: a cross for idle, a filled square for the Phase 1 accuracy screen, a hollow circle for the last member of a group that fell below two, a hollow triangle for players removed because fewer than two groups were left), so a group removed at the screen is three squares in the gap between the phases. The reason categories are those of `stats/R/attrition.R`, and `player_table()` is the one place the figures code them. Games are rows of nine players and a gap, ordered by outcome (complete, three groups to the end, two groups, ended early), then by players lost and when the first left; the four condition panels are one figure, each under its condition's name, with one key for the reasons below (the group colors are left to the caption). The same reasons stack as bars of removals per block in `REMOVAL_SHADES`, ink to light gray, with the screen as its own bar between blocks 6 and 7 (`attrition_curves.py`), beside step curves of the games still fully intact (solid) and still running (dashed, `BETWEEN_LINESTYLE`) by condition, counted out of the thirty started, the two panels one figure on `CURVES_PANEL_AXES`, each with its key to its right, centered on the plot (the conditions, the reasons) and the games panel naming its two lines in a key at its lower left, where no curve reaches.
- The fate of each group's Phase 1 convention (`convention_fates.py`) is read over the final three blocks of Phase 2 with the word coding above: shared where its own group and another group both used it there, kept where only its own group did, taken over where only another group did, gone where nobody did, "used" meaning at least one description carrying one of its words, and a convention counted only where its own group described the tangram in the window. The fates stack as one bar per condition on `COLUMNS_PANEL_AXES` in the stack alphas, the two fates in which the group still uses the convention at the bottom, gone in `ABSENT` gray at the top, each segment carrying its share and the four fates named beside the last bar, capitalized, each with its gloss in parentheses below it and a line to its segment; in `refer_separated` the shared share is the coding's floor, since its groups never meet.
- A listener's two answer rates (`listener_answer_rates.py`), the share of in-group and of out-group speakers judged in-group over the judgments made in time, are a pair of columns per social condition on `COLUMNS_PANEL_AXES`, drawn with `draw_column()`, the single column that `draw_columns()` repeats: listeners as `RESPONDENT_POINT`, the mean and its interval over games as the black line and box, chance at one half, the columns ticked by the speaker's group and each pair under its condition's name.
- A schematic of a participant's screen (`listener_screens.py`) is simplified, not a reproduction of the app: it keeps what the participant acts on (the instruction, the grid, the speaker's description and, on the social screen, the group question and its answers) and leaves out the header, the score, the group card and the avatars, with the layout following the app's only roughly. It is drawn on `SCREEN_PANEL_AXES` in the screen's own units, y running downward, from a real trial of the example pair: the grid in the order the server dealt that listener, with the target outlined in ink, and the trio's chat. The wording is copied from the client source and `data()` refuses to run when a string no longer appears there; a stimulus in a grid is drawn with `draw_stimulus()`, and the whole screen stays neutral gray and ink, with the added element outlined in ink rather than a condition color.
