# Figures

The article's figures are drawn by the modules in `report/figures/`, one module per figure, each named for what it shows (`group_specificity.py`, never a figure number, because the numbers change while the article is written and the manuscript's `\includegraphics` maps the number to the name). `report/plot_style.py` is the one description of how the figures look (palette, labels, type sizes, mark styles, figure and panel sizes, the broken block axis) and `report/figures/_common.py` holds the reading, the two summaries over games and the drawing idioms built on it; a module defines none of its own, so a change in either changes every figure. A figure the article no longer needs is removed with its module and its PDF; the modules are the figures the article uses, not a library of past ones. What one module shows, where its data come from and why it is drawn as it is are in that module's docstring and the comments on its constants; this rule holds what is true across modules.

## The contract

A module defines `data(dirs)`, the long table the figure plots, read from the processed tables, the measures, the model inputs in `data/<dataset>/model_inputs/` and the estimates in `results/estimates/`, and `plot(df)`, the figure drawn from that table alone. An aggregate a model also uses belongs in preprocessing, not here. A module whose panels the SI draws again on a subset of games gives `data()` an optional `restriction`, the name of a rerun of `stats/primary/robustness.R` as `primary/robustness.csv` names it (`RESTRICTIONS` in `_common.py`, so far `complete_games`): the table then holds the games the rerun keeps and the tests' rows are the rerun's; the runner itself always calls `data(dirs)`. The runner (`make figures`, or `uv run python -m report.figures --dataset full [name ...]`) calls both for every module and writes `results/plots/<name>.pdf` with no creation date, so an unchanged figure is byte-identical from run to run and `git diff results/plots/` is the regression check. The panels are inputs to Illustrator, not to the manuscript: the author assembles the figures there and exports them to `figures/paper_files/`, which `make sync` copies into the manuscript's project. A figure the author assembles by hand returns a dict from `plot()`, one figure per panel keyed by what the panel shows, written as `results/plots/<name>_<key>.pdf`; a module that sets `FOLDER` writes into that subfolder instead (`confirmatory_tests/` for the figures of the preregistered tests, `intact_games/` for the main panels drawn on the complete games). The tests are in `report/test_figures.py` and `report/test_figure_common.py`.

## The look

Uncertainty is a percentile-bootstrap 95% interval over games (`report/bootstrap.py` resamples games and pools their rows, so the point estimate is the mean over observations; the semantic-drift interval in `exploratory.tex` is one), with the games as thin lines or points behind the mean, also where a game's per-block means are noisy, and the y range holds every game's line. A cumulative distribution (the continuity panel) is pooled over a condition's pairs with no interval, each game's own distribution a thin line behind it. No gridlines, open black axes, one plot per figure, sentence-case labels, and no key on a panel: the author adds one legend per figure in Illustrator and resizes the panels there, so a panel names no condition, line style or category and reserves no room for one, its PDF trimmed to what it draws (`bbox_inches="tight"`, `pad_inches=0.05`, every axis label counted in full, since the tight bounding box alone cuts a label longer than its axis). The one exception is a panel whose lines differ by their names alone, the alignment outcomes, which names its lines with `label_lines()`. The SI figures (`attrition_timelines.py`, `attrition_curves.py`, `convention_fates.py`, `listener_answer_rates.py`) are complete figures rather than panels and draw their condition names and their keys or direct labels themselves; `intact_games.py`, the SI's redrawing of the main panels on the complete games, follows the main panels' rules, since its panels are the main modules' own. A panel keeps its axis and tick labels, reference-line labels, and text that is data, such as the conventions written beside the example strands. Game counts are left to the caption, never written into a label or a legend; where a caption gives one it is written as "16 games", never "n = 16", which readers take as participants. Never draw a line for a quantity a condition does not have: `refer_separated` has no out-group speakers. The game-block axis is broken between the phases (`add_phase_boundary()`), each phase drawn as its own segment so that no line crosses the gap.

## Panels assembled by hand

The author assembles multi-panel figures in Illustrator from single-panel PDFs. Such panels share a plotting area through `panel_figure()`: `PANEL_AXES` for a panel of a row, `BLOCK_PANEL_AXES` for one that spans all twelve blocks, ticked with `SPARSE_BLOCK_TICKS`, `LARGE_BLOCK_PANEL_AXES`, larger and ticked at every block, for a block panel that leads its figure (the similarity trajectories of `group_specificity.py`), and `SQUARE_PANEL_AXES` for a panel of Phase 2 outcomes, one quantity against another with a point per game or a trajectory over blocks 7 to 12 alone (`PHASE2_RANGE`, ticked with `PHASE2_TICKS`, a plain axis rather than the broken one with an empty Phase 1); the panel is small and the type is at its usual size, so that the type survives the panel being scaled down in print. Every other kind of panel has its own named plotting area in `plot_style.py` (`COLUMNS_PANEL_AXES` for a column per condition, `STACK_PANEL_AXES` for stacked shares over blocks, `ROWS_PANEL_AXES` and `TANGRAM_ROWS_PANEL_AXES` for a row per game or per tangram, `SCREEN_PANEL_AXES` for a participant's screen, `ATTRITION_PANEL_AXES` and `CURVES_PANEL_AXES` for the SI's attrition figures), and a new kind of panel gets its own rather than borrowing one; a tall panel that is reduced further than its neighbors to sit beside them sets its type a step larger (`permutation_null.py`). A panel carries no letter, no note and no title of any kind, since the letters, notes and keys are added in Illustrator and the legend says which game and condition a panel shows; the margins hold only what the panel carries outside the axes. The panels of a row share their plotting area: the block panels are wider than tall, the Phase 2 panels square.

## Settled conventions

- Verbatim descriptions in a figure are wrapped over lines, never cut.
- Marks are filled before the manipulation and hollow after it (`PHASE_POINT`, or `ROW_POINT` on a figure with one row per game or per tangram): the face the series color or white, the edge the series color.
- On the broken block axis, marks set side by side within a block are dodged with `dodge()`, by points, never by a fraction of a block, which the gap would stretch.
- A stimulus drawn in a panel (`stimulus_glyph()`, or `stimulus_row_labels()` beside a figure's rows) sits in headroom or margin reserved for it, where no mark or label can reach it.
- Where two kinds of line share a panel and the color carries the condition (within- and between-group similarity, in-group and out-group speakers), the first is solid and the second is `BETWEEN_LINESTYLE`. Where such lines carry points, the second kind's are filled triangles (`BETWEEN_MARKER`), never hollow circles, which mean Phase 2.
- The example games are fixed in `EXAMPLES` of `convention_examples.py`, two pairs of a refer_mixed and a social_mixed game matched on the phase before the manipulation, with the matching rule stated in the legend, and every other example panel (the contact network, the convention spread, the convention maps, the listener screens) draws from them, so that one game is followed across figures; every game of the two conditions is drawn as thin lines behind an example's curves.
- One quantity against another over games (`draw_scatter()` on a `scatter_table()`) sets a point per game behind each condition's mean, a larger point with its two intervals as a cross, and carries no fitted line; where the conditions' points overlap, `shapes=True` gives each condition its shape from `CONDITION_MARKERS` as well as its color, with the game points a little larger (`SHAPED_GAME_POINT`), while lines never carry markers. The Phase 2 scatter panels share the group-specificity axis `SPECIFICITY_LIMITS`, and a game appears only with both quantities.
- A column per condition (`draw_columns()`, or `draw_column()` for one column) holds games (`GAME_POINT`) or individual respondents (`RESPONDENT_POINT`, lighter) as jittered points, the jitter seeded so the PDF reproduces, under the condition's mean as a black line across the column and its bootstrap interval as the outline of a box around the line (`RESPONDENT_MEAN`, `RESPONDENT_INTERVAL`), with the condition names as the tick labels in ink.
- A network of a game's players sets them on a circle in group order with `network_layout()`, the same layout in every panel of the same game, a node per player in its group's color (`NETWORK_NODE`, or `RINGED_NODE` where the rim carries the group and the face something else) and edges of one width in translucent ink (`NETWORK_EDGE`); what an edge counts goes in the legend, never in its width, and a node carries no label. Where the other players could not see a player's identity (Phase 2 of a mixed condition), the node is gray with a dotted ring in its group's color (`MASKED_NODE`). A player appears in a figure's table as a node index, never as an id.
- Stacked shares are areas or bars in steps of the condition's color (`STACK_ALPHAS`), divided by thin white lines, an absence (a convention nobody uses) in `ABSENT` gray, and carry no interval.
- A reference line's label sits above its right end, or its left end (`reference_line(..., side="left")`) where the data reach the right one; every chance line is labeled "chance".
- A model estimate is read from `results/estimates/`, never recomputed in a figure: a per-game group-specificity endpoint comes from `specificity_endpoints()` (the sentence-embedding estimates of `stats/primary/group_specificity.R` where estimable), the permutation range from the Phase 1 check's `perm_lower` and `perm_upper`, and a contrast or p-value from the estimates by `contrast_estimates()`.
- A measure a figure computes itself is descriptive, says so in its docstring, and has no model or macro. The two codings of the Phase 1 conventions are in `_common.py` with their definitions as their docstrings: `phase1_conventions()`, a group's convention for a tangram as the unit mean embedding of its members' final Phase 1 descriptions, and the word coding `convention_vocabulary()` with `code_convention_use()`. A measure a model also uses belongs in preprocessing.
- A figure of a preregistered test shows the data the test models, one point per game in a column per condition, with the planned contrast as a bracket marked by `significance_stars()` from the estimates' two-tailed p-value and, where the contrasts share a scale, a panel of them with their model intervals (`draw_contrasts()`) labelled by their predicted direction ("Separated > mixed"). Nothing is refit, and a derived scale (the standardized differences of `confirmatory_summary.py`) is descriptive while the marks stay the tests'.
- A panel drawn again on a subset of games (`intact_games.py`) is its source module's panel from `data(dirs, restriction=...)`, the same code on the games the rerun keeps, which `intact_games()` codes as the pipeline does (`complete_nine` of `report/descriptives.py`) and the modules check against the rerun's game counts. Its marks are the rerun's, and a test no script reruns carries no mark rather than the full sample's, which is why `significance_stars()` refuses a missing p-value.
- The reason categories for a player's removal are those of `stats/R/attrition.R`, coded once for the figures in `player_table()` of `attrition_timelines.py` and drawn as marks (`REMOVAL_MARKERS`) or stacked shades (`REMOVAL_SHADES`).
- Text a figure quotes from the experiment (a survey question, a screen's instruction) is copied from the client source, and `data()` refuses to run when the string no longer appears there (`exit_survey.py`, `listener_screens.py`).
